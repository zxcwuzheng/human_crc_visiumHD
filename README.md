## Abstract
Bulk transcriptomic studies have yielded abundant colorectal cancer (CRC) biomarkers. However, their clinical translation is hindered by tissue homogenization, which obscures their spatial origins. Here, we present an integrative framework leveraging spatial transcriptomics to spatially resolve and repurpose legacy CRC bulk biomarkers. First, spatially projecting of 1,188 bulk biomarkers revealed pronounced spatial heterogeneity. By integrating co-expression and protein-protein interaction networks, we successfully partitioned bulk biomarkers into distinct modules anchored to specific microenvironmental regions and identified key network hubs. Spatial ligand-receptor interaction analysis further established CD44 and COL1A1 as both network hubs and central mediators of spatial communication. In silico gene knockout suggested that CD44 may regulate epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and ferroptosis programs, while COL1A1 drives extracellular matrix remodeling. Finally, an independent cohort confirmed the spatial origins and spatial communications of these hub biomarkers at true single-cell resolution. In conclusion, our analytical framework spatially resolved established bulk biomarkers, providing a scalable strategy to prioritize key drivers and therapeutic targets at the spatial level.
